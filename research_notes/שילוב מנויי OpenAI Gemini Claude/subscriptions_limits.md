# Consumer/Pro AI Subscription Tiers (OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude): what ~$500 / $200 / $200 a month buys, limits, and ToS (as of Oct 12, 2026)

> Method note: direct fetches of official pages (chatgpt.com, developers.openai.com, claude.com) were blocked by the sandbox network policy, so these findings come from web-search results. They include snippets of official pages (help.openai.com, support.claude.com, gemini.google, one.google.com) plus tech press and third-party blogs. Third-party blogs conflicted often, especially on model names and exact quotas. Each conflict is flagged where it occurs. Dates are given wherever known.

## OpenAI: tiers, what $500/month most plausibly is, Codex, Deep Research, Agent, Sora, models

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 ChatGPT has three Pro tiers: Pro 100 ($100), Pro 200 ($200) and Pro 500 ($500). Pro 500 launched in late September 2026. A $500/month OpenAI spend therefore most plausibly means one **ChatGPT Pro 500** subscription rather than Pro plus API credits or multiple seats. Pro 500 has the highest included usage, including Codex, and is the only tier with "Ultrafast" models. Codex has no standalone subscription. It is bundled into ChatGPT plans, and API-key use is billed per token.

### Inferences
- The most likely reading of "$500/month to OpenAI" is a single ChatGPT Pro 500 seat. Before late Sept 2026 the same money would have meant something like Pro $200 plus about $300 of API spend, or several Plus/Business seats. Pro 500 is now the natural single-user choice for heavy Codex users, especially because Pro 200's allowance shrinks for new subscribers and, from Oct 29, for legacy ones.
- OpenAI is currently the most permissive of the three about using a subscription login in third-party harnesses. That makes a ChatGPT Pro subscription usable as a "backend" for orchestrators such as OpenClaw or OpenCode through Codex OAuth.

### Gaps

- The current flagship model lineup and names ("GPT-6 / 6.1 Sol / Astra" vs "GPT-5.6") could not be confirmed from a primary source.

## Google: AI Pro vs AI Ultra, what $200/month is, and Gemini/Antigravity/Jules/Deep Think/Veo limits

### Takeaway
Since Google I/O (May 19–20, 2026), Google AI Ultra has two tiers. Ultra 5x costs $99.99/mo and Ultra 20x costs $199.99/mo, down from the old single $249.99 Ultra. So **$200/month to Google means the Ultra 20x tier** (20x AI Pro usage limits). AI Pro is $19.99. Gemini CLI consumer login was shut off on June 18, 2026 and replaced by the Antigravity CLI (`agy`).

## Terms of service, recent crackdowns, and subscription vs API cost-effectiveness

### Takeaway
Policies diverge sharply.
- **OpenAI** openly endorses ChatGPT/Codex OAuth in third-party tools.
- **Anthropic** banned subscription use in third-party harnesses (Apr 2026), then partially restored it through Agent SDK paths and monthly API credits (Oct 2026).
- **Google** banned Antigravity-OAuth use in OpenClaw (Feb 2026) and shut down Gemini CLI consumer login (June 18, 2026), so third-party Gemini use effectively requires an API key.

Subscriptions are far cheaper than API pay-as-you-go for heavy agentic coding. That gap is exactly why the providers are restricting third-party harnesses.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenAI** supports Codex subscription OAuth in OpenClaw and other external tools. — [OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/providers/openai); [explainx](https://explainx.ai/blog/openclaw-chatgpt-plus-pro-openai-anthropic-subscription-2026)
- **Anthropic** started with an April 4, 2026 ban that was later softened with the Oct 7, 2026 API credits. The stated rationale was "outsized strain", with subscribers consuming far more tokens than their plans cost. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/04/anthropic-says-claude-code-subscribers-will-need-to-pay-extra-for-openclaw-support/); [annielytics timeline](https://www.annielytics.com/tools/ai-timeline/company/openclaw/); [support.claude.com](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15036540-use-the-claude-agent-sdk-with-your-claude-plan)
- **Google** issued Feb 2026 suspensions over OpenClaw and Antigravity OAuth. Google's DeepMind product lead said the surge "tremendously degraded the quality of service". — [secure.com](https://www.secure.com/blog/cybersecurity/google-bans-openclaw-users-a-warning-shot-for-the-agentic-ai-era); [mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/google-enforces-tos-bans-on-paid-antigravity-subscribers-using-openclaw-tool/)
- **Size of the subsidy.** One analysis of Anthropic's May 2026 plan estimated a "12x–175x effective price increase by workload" if subscription agent usage moved to API-rate credits. This implies heavy users get far more than list-price value from subscriptions. — [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/MagnaCapax/d9177e35b355853f03c730dfcaa693ef)

### Inferences
- **Cost-effectiveness.** For sustained interactive or agentic coding, flat subscriptions (Pro 500, Ultra 20x, Max 20x) beat API pay-as-you-go by large multiples, per the 12x–175x estimate above. The API is better for bursty, automated, parallel or production workloads, for anything Google outside its first-party surfaces, and wherever ToS forbids subscription OAuth.
- **Orchestrating all three subscriptions** (for example, one harness driving Codex, Claude Code and Gemini):
  - The safest ToS-compliant pattern is to invoke each vendor's **own first-party CLI or agent**: Codex CLI with ChatGPT login, Claude Code / Agent SDK / `claude -p`, and Antigravity CLI (`agy`). Avoid extracting OAuth tokens into third-party clients.
  - OpenAI tolerates the latter. Google explicitly forbids it.

### Gaps
- No formal OpenAI ToS clause on third-party OAuth was found. Support is evidenced by docs and statements, not a policy page.
- Whether Google's ToS permits a third-party orchestrator to shell out to the official `agy` binary under a consumer login is unclear. No source addressed it.
