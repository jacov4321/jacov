# Legal and Practical Risk Framework for Naming an OS / AI Product "LYLA" (Israeli developer, international sales, as of Oct 2026)

> General information only, not legal advice. Fees and timelines change; confirm on uspto.gov, gov.il (ILPO) and wipo.int, and have a qualified Israeli/US trademark attorney run a full clearance before launch. Note: no live register search (USPTO/EUIPO/ILPO) for "LYLA" was run in this research. The web searches done here did not turn up a LYLA registration, but that is not clearance.

## Key Question 1: Likelihood-of-confusion tests (US DuPont, EU, Israel), class 9/42 overlap, and how strong a first name (Lyla) is as a mark

### Takeaway
All three systems look at the marks and the goods/services as a whole, and a strong match on one factor can make up for a weaker match on the other. The likely outcome is that LYLA for OS/AI software (class 9 + 42) would be blocked by any earlier identical or near-identical mark (LYLA, LAYLA, LILA, LYLAH) on software or IT services. A common first name gives a weak mark: it is fairly easy to register, but its scope of protection is narrow.

### Cited Findings
- US: TMEP §1207.01 applies the DuPont factors. The two key factors are (1) how similar the marks are in their entireties, "as to appearance, sound, connotation and commercial impression", and (2) how related the goods are. Not every factor applies in every case. — [BitLaw TMEP 1207.01](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1207_01.html); [Waltmire on DuPont factors](https://www.waltmire.com/2020/03/08/trademark-office-likelihood-of-confusion-factors-the-dupont-factors/)
- US relatedness: the goods do not have to be identical. It is enough that they are "related in some manner and/or if the circumstances surrounding their marketing are such that they could give rise to the mistaken belief that they emanate from the same source". Examiners rely on internet/database evidence that the goods are used together or by the same buyers. — [BitLaw TMEP 1207.01](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1207_01.html)
- US, in practice: TTAB examiners treat software identifications that overlap a cited registration as "identical-in-part". — [TTAB opinion via ttabvue](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-86354701-EXA-14.pdf) (gap: the exact TMEP text on software, the 1207.01(a) subsections, was not retrieved).
- US mark strength: the Federal Circuit treats strength as "not a binary factor". It runs on a spectrum and is measured by conceptual and marketplace strength. A "crowded field" of similar third-party marks on similar goods narrows protection (e.g., CAREFIRST v. FIRSTCAROLINACARE weighed how weak "CARE" and "FIRST" were). Weak marks can still register, and even a weak mark can still win an opposition when the marks are very similar. — [Clark Hill on weak marks](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/worried-your-trademark-might-be-commercially-and-conceptually-weak-recent-opinions-by-the-trademark-trial-and-appeal-board-indicate-that-could-be-advantageous/); [Sierra IP Law on crowded fields](https://sierraiplaw.com/trademark-crowded-field/)
- EU: Article 8(1)(b) EUTMR looks at whether the marks and the goods/services are identical or similar, and includes likelihood of association. The assessment is global and interdependent: a lower degree of similarity in goods can be offset by a higher similarity in marks, and the other way round. — [ferenda T-472/17 summary](https://ferenda.lagen.nu/celex/62017TJ0472); [ie-forum, PAPYROS BY MODUS v PAPYRUS, Sept 2026](https://ie-forum.nl/artikelen/gerecht-verwarringsgevaar-tussen-papyros-by-modus-en-ouder-merk-papyrus-voor-software-en-it-diensten)
- EU class 9/42 overlap: in the Sept 2026 PAPYROS/PAPYRUS software case, the General Court found an average degree of similarity between class 9 goods and at least a low degree of similarity between class 42 services and the earlier class 9 software. Together with high phonetic and conceptual similarity, that was enough for likelihood of confusion. — [ie-forum](https://ie-forum.nl/artikelen/gerecht-verwarringsgevaar-tussen-papyros-by-modus-en-ouder-merk-papyrus-voor-software-en-it-diensten)
- EU: in COLLIBRA v KOLIBRI (T-128/20, T-129/20, 2021), confusion was found for data-governance software (class 9) and related class 42 services, based on conceptual and phonetic closeness. — [Jacobacci](https://www.jacobacci.com/en/news/trademarks-collibra-and-kolibri)
- Israel: courts use a "triple test": (1) sight and sound, (2) type of goods and customers / trade channels, and (3) all other circumstances. A "common sense" test supplements it, and marks are compared as wholes. — [DWO on the triple test](https://dwo.co.il/?p=17301); [Salomon v. Yaasin (Cardozo Versa)](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/477)
- Israel: sight and sound has been called the most important prong. In one online-services dispute, the Supreme Court gave appearance priority over sound. — [Mondaq "Confusing or not confusing"](https://admin.mondaq.com/trademark/1218824/confusing-or-not-confusing-that-is-the-question)
- Israel (2025): reportedly, the Trademarks Commissioner has widened the confusion analysis beyond point-of-sale confusion, and Israeli courts recognize "endorsement confusion", meaning consumers assume the trademark owner approved the product. — [Liad Whatstein update, March 10 2025 (search snippet only; page could not be fetched)](https://liadwhatstein.com/tm-update-iltmo-reevaluates-and-expands-the-tests-for-determining-likelihood-of-confusion/)
- Existing near-identical name in AI: "Layla" is an offline AI companion app from Layla Network Pty Ltd (Southport, Queensland, Australia), first released 9 Aug 2023. Whether it is registered as a trademark was not confirmed. — [Software Advice listing](https://www.softwareadvice.com.au/software/636054/Layla); [PromptQuorum review](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/layla-review)

### Inferences
- LYLA vs LAYLA vs LILA vs LYLAH differ by one letter and sound almost or exactly the same. In all three regimes, identical or closely related software goods would probably push the result toward confusion. The "Layla" AI app is therefore a real conflict candidate in the AI-assistant space, especially in Australia, the EU and the US if it has filed anywhere.
- Class 9 (downloadable software / OS) and class 42 (SaaS, software design) are routinely treated as related, so a prior mark in either class blocks the other.
- Lyla is a common given name, so it probably sits in a crowded field. That cuts both ways: the applicant will find it easier to coexist with marks in different fields, but will find it harder to stop others later. It is a "registrable but weak" choice. Because it is a first name, it is not descriptive of software, so it should be inherently distinctive (arbitrary) for software. The weakness is commercial and comes from crowding, not conceptual.

### Gaps
- No live search of the USPTO, EUIPO (TMview) or ILPO registers for LYLA / LAYLA / LILA in classes 9/42 was done. This is the single most important missing fact.
- The exact TMEP text on software relatedness and the EUIPO Guidelines text on class 9/42 comparisons were not retrieved.
- No TTAB decision dealing specifically with given-name (not surname) marks was found. A first name is not refused under §2(e)(4) as "primarily merely a surname" unless it is also a surname. Lyla is not known as a surname, but this is unverified.
- The text of the Israeli Trademarks Ordinance 1972 (relative grounds, including well-known marks) was not retrieved.

## Key Question 2: Can a Disney/Marvel fictional character name (LYLA, the AI in Spider-Man 2099 / Across the Spider-Verse) block a software/OS mark? Precedents, dilution, and the "AI assistant" semantic risk

### Takeaway
A character name can be protected as a trademark when it works as a source identifier, through registration or through acquired distinctiveness (the Krusty Krab case). Disney, Marvel and Lucasfilm do actively oppose filings that match their characters. No Marvel registration for "LYLA" was found, and dilution claims need household-name fame, which LYLA almost certainly lacks. The realistic risk is therefore a cease-and-desist or an opposition on confusion / false-endorsement grounds, made worse because the product would be an AI assistant just as the character is, rather than a likely loss in court. That makes the risk moderate and centered on the AI-assistant positioning, not fatal.

### Cited Findings
- LYLA (LYrate Lifeform Approximation) is the AI assistant of Miguel O'Hara / Spider-Man 2099. She was created by Peter David and Rick Leonardi in Spider-Man 2099 #1 and is voiced by Greta Lee in Across the Spider-Verse (2023). — [Wikipedia: Spider-Man 2099](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_2099); [SlashFilm on LYLA](https://slashfilm.com/1329249/spider-man-2099-lyla-across-the-spider-verse-halo-the-flintstones)
- Marvel Entertainment Group holds Spider-Man 2099 marks (e.g., SPIDER-MAN 2099, filed 24 Sept 1992, for comic books). A Justia owner listing excerpt showed no LYLA filing. — [Justia: Marvel Entertainment Group trademarks](https://trademark.justia.com/owners/marvel-entertainment-group-inc-26696/page3)
- Character elements can be trademarks. In Viacom v. IJR Capital (5th Cir. 2018), the fictional "Krusty Krab" restaurant was protected without a registration because it played a central role in the show and had acquired distinctiveness through 18+ years of the show, licensing and merchandise. A real restaurant using the name was found likely to cause confusion as to source, affiliation or sponsorship. — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/fifth-circuit-affirms-tm-protection-for-spongebobs); [Lexplug case brief](https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/viacom_international_inc_v_ijr_capital_investments_l_l_c__65b5ba7170bbed53e94b8f46)
- Commentators have criticized Krusty Krab for not asking "secondary meaning in what goods or services?" — [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=18858)
- Disney "Teen Tink": Disney opposed United's TEEN TINK on priority and likelihood of confusion with TINKER BELL (registered for dolls/toys). The TTAB and the Federal Circuit found confusion likely, even though the Tinker Bell character is in the public domain. — [Trademark Lawyer Firm](https://www.trademarklawyerfirm.com/disney-intellectual-property-issues-uniteds-teen-tink-trademark-rejected/)
- Marvel Characters, Inc. routinely files extensions of time to oppose marks it sees as touching its brands, e.g., SUPERHERO FIRE PROTECTION (2012) and SUPERHERO CHALLENGE (2016). — [TTABVUE 85606597](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-85606597-EXT-2.pdf); [TTABVUE 86907829](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-86907829-EXT-2.pdf)
- Marvel weighed opposing "Wacoinda" (crypto) and "Wakanda Wine Fest", which recall the fictional country Wakanda. The outcome was not found. — [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/marvel-mulls-black-panther-cryptocurrency-tm-opposition-16463)
- Lucasfilm (Disney) examples:
  - THE FORCE for card games: the TTAB sustained Lucasfilm's opposition in 2002, then vacated it after the applicant abandoned with consent. — [USPTO OG notice 2003](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/com/sol/og/2003/week02/patttab.htm)
  - Lucasfilm sued over the "Jedi Mind" brainwave software/headset, although it held no "Jedi Mind" mark. — [Slashdot](https://yro.slashdot.org/story/10/08/25/0436219/LucasFilm-Sues-Jedi-Mind-Over-Jedi); [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/technology/lucas-sues-to-stop-jedi-mind-headset/article1378048)
  - Australia refused BIODROIDS based on Lucasfilm's reputation in "droid". — [POF Australia](https://pof.com.au/?p=3223)
  - Lucasfilm petitioned to cancel a SABACC registration (a fictional card game). — [TTABVUE 92066016](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-92066016-CAN-1.pdf)
- Comic-character analogy outside Disney: in Germany, MobiliX (a Linux-on-mobile project) lost a court case to the publisher of the Obelix comics because the marks were similar, and was renamed TuxMobil. — [FSFE list 2003](https://list2.fsfe.org/hyperkitty/list/discussion@lists.fsfe.org/thread/NKHOKTJDMVL753BPVN2A2I6ZUBY5KBXR)
- US dilution (TDRA): the plaintiff must show the mark is "widely recognized by the general consuming public" so that the public associates the term with the owner "in almost any context". In Coach Services v. Triumph Learning (Fed. Cir. 2012), even COACH failed the fame test. Dilution fame is "reserved for those few brands that have become household names". — [Harvard JOLT](https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/digest/coach-servs-inc-v-triumph-learning-l-l-c); [INTA](https://www.inta.org/?p=5753); [Olshan](https://olshanlaw.com/Advertising-Law-Blog/Trademark)

### Inferences
- Dilution is a very weak theory for Marvel. LYLA is a supporting character, recognized mainly by fans of the comics and films, and nowhere near "household name" fame among the general consuming public. "Niche fame" is not enough after the 2006 TDRA. This last point is background knowledge and should be checked against 15 U.S.C. §1125(c).
- Confusion and false endorsement are the real vectors. Marvel licenses Spider-Verse merchandise and apps, and the character IS an AI assistant. A product called "LYLA" marketed as an AI assistant/OS could support a sponsorship or affiliation theory (as in Krusty Krab, and Israel's "endorsement confusion"), especially if the marketing uses holographic-assistant imagery, a female AI persona, Spider-Verse aesthetics or the "Lyrate Lifeform" wordplay. Plain "LYLA" as a generic first name for a non-assistant OS is lower risk.
- Without a Marvel registration for LYLA, Marvel would have to prove common-law rights and secondary meaning in the name for relevant goods. That is harder than for Krusty Krab, because LYLA has far less standalone merchandising. It can still be costly for a small developer: a Disney cease-and-desist or opposition is commonly settled by renaming rather than fought.
- Watch-service risk: Disney/Marvel monitor USPTO publications, as their routine extension-of-time filings show. A US application for LYLA in class 9/42 is the step most likely to trigger contact.

### Gaps
- Whether Marvel/Disney has filed or registered "LYLA" in any jurisdiction (US, EU, Israel) is unverified. A TESS/tmsearch, TMview and ILPO search is needed.
- No decided case was found in which a minor comic character's name blocked an unrelated software mark. The Teen Tink, Krusty Krab and Droid cases involve far more famous elements.
- Disney/Marvel opposition outcomes against software/app marks specifically were not found.

## Key Question 3: Precedents of OS / open-source / software projects forced to rename over trademarks

### Takeaway
Renames are common and usually happen early, often triggered by an incumbent in the same or a neighboring software category, not by a court loss. They show that the realistic cost is a forced rebrand, not damages.

### Cited Findings
- Phoenix to Firebird to Firefox: Mozilla's browser dropped "Phoenix" on 14 April 2003 because of a trademark dispute with Phoenix Technologies, the BIOS maker, which had a browser called Phoenix FirstWare Connect. "Firebird" then clashed with the Firebird database project, whose community campaigned against it. "Firefox" was chosen in February 2004, and Mozilla then registered it. — [Firefox early history (Wikipedia mirror)](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox_1); [Stanford CS case study](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/cs181/projects/firefox-market-dynamics/index.html)
- Hudson to Jenkins (2011): Oracle claimed the Hudson trademark. The CI community forked and renamed to Jenkins because Oracle's assurances were not binding. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2011/01/11/hudson_renamed_jenkins/)
- MobiliX to TuxMobil (Germany, 2003): lost to the Obelix comic publisher's mark. — [FSFE list](https://list2.fsfe.org/hyperkitty/list/discussion@lists.fsfe.org/thread/NKHOKTJDMVL753BPVN2A2I6ZUBY5KBXR)
- OpenStack Quantum to Neutron, and Marconi to Zaqar, were both renamed over trademark issues (mailing list and blueprint sources, secondary). — [OpenStack-dev 2016](https://lists.openstack.org/pipermail/openstack-dev/2016-April/091125.html); [Launchpad blueprint](https://blueprints.qastaging.launchpad.net/python-zaqarclient/+spec/project-rename)
- Jade to Pug (Node template engine): renamed after a company holding "Jade" objected. This is a secondhand forum account. — [yhetil guix thread](https://yhetil.org/guix/mum1t6uy.fsf@yandex.com)
- Reverse example: in 2013 Groupon abandoned its "Gnome" applications after pushback from the GNOME Foundation and community. — [ZDNet](https://www.zdnet.com.au/article/groupon-retreats-from-claim-on-gnome-foundation-trademarks/)
- An Israeli court example involving an OS brand: Red Hat failed to protect its Fedora logo in a Tel Aviv court (2011). — [law.co.il](https://www.law.co.il/en/articles/2011/07/19/red-hat-fails-to-protec-fedora-logo-in-tel-aviv-court/)

### Inferences
- The Firebird episode is the most instructive for LYLA. Mozilla, a well-resourced organization, renamed twice in about 10 months because neither name was cleared against neighboring software marks. A full search before launch would have avoided both.
- Most renames came from software-industry rights holders. The comic-publisher case (Obelix) shows that entertainment owners also win against software projects in some jurisdictions.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for any Linux Mint trademark conflict, and the "Mint" example could not be verified, so it should not be cited.
- The Jade/Pug and Quantum/Neutron details rest on secondhand posts.

## Key Question 4: Practical clearance process, costs and timelines (Israel, Madrid, USPTO)

### Takeaway
A typical path is: free knockout search (USPTO, TMview/EUIPO, ILPO, WIPO Global Brand Database, app stores, GitHub, domains), then a paid full search with an attorney opinion, then an Israeli filing in classes 9 + 42, then a Madrid international application based on it (designating US, EU, etc.) within the 6-month Paris priority window. Official fees alone run roughly ₪2,800 in Israel for two classes plus a few thousand CHF for Madrid. Attorney fees come on top of that.

### Cited Findings
- USPTO (since 18 Jan 2025, with no newer change found):
  - The TEAS Plus/Standard split is gone. There is a single base application fee of $350 per class.
  - Surcharges per class: +$200 if the goods/services are not taken verbatim from the ID Manual; +$100 for missing required information; +$200 for each extra 1,000 characters of free-form text.
  - Sections 8/9 cost $325 per class, and Section 15 costs $250 per class.
  - [Fish & Richardson](https://www.fr.com/insights/thought-leadership/blogs/new-uspto-trademark-fees-go-into-effect-january-18-2025/); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/usptos-significant-restructuring-trademark-fees-takes-effect-january-18-2025); [Gerben Law](https://www.gerbenlaw.com/?p=68568)
  - The 2025 sources put the US Madrid (§66(a)) fee at $600 per class. — [Gerben Law summary via search](https://www.gerbenlaw.com/?p=68568). This is a US-internal figure; the Madrid individual fee for designating the US is paid in CHF and should be checked in the WIPO calculator.
- Israel (ILPO) filing fees, from practitioner sources that disagree with one another:
  - ₪1,604 for the first class and ₪1,205 for each additional class — [DWO](https://dwo.co.il/how-much-does-it-cost-to-register-a-trademark-in-israel/)
  - ₪1,623 / ₪1,219 — [Mondaq comparative guide](https://www.mondaq.com/ip/788898/trademarks-comparative-guide)
  - ₪1,795 / ₪1,350 — [lawoffice.org.il](https://lawoffice.org.il/en/cost-of-registering-a-trademark-in-israel/)
  - About ₪1,500 per class — [tmarkmetric, June 2026](https://tmarkmetric.com/guides/israel-trademark)
  - Expedited examination costs about ₪750 per class. — [DWO](https://dwo.co.il/how-much-does-it-cost-to-register-a-trademark-in-israel/)
- Israel timeline:
  - First examination takes about 4 months according to the IP Commissioner in an INTA interview (undated). — [INTA](https://www.inta.org/perspectives/israels-ip-commissioner-on-the-changing-landscape-and-promoting-entrepreneurship/)
  - Practitioners say about 12 to 15 months. — [lawoffice.org.il](https://lawoffice.org.il/en/time-to-register-a-trademark-in-israel/); [DWO](https://dwo.co.il/trademarks-prosecution-process-in-israel/)
  - After acceptance comes a 3-month opposition period that cannot be extended. — [WTR 2019](https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/global-guide/the-wtr-yearbook/2019/article/trademark-procedures-and-strategies-israel-0)
  - Expedited examination needs an affidavit and is discretionary. — [DWO](https://dwo.co.il/trademarks-prosecution-process-in-israel/)
- WIPO Madrid:
  - Basic fee is 653 CHF (black and white) or 903 CHF (colour).
  - Complementary fee is 100 CHF per designated member, or an individual fee where a member has set one.
  - [WIPO fees page](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/how_to/file/fees); [WIPO schedule of fees](https://www.wipo.int/madrid/en/fees/sched.html) (the schedule shown is dated 1 Feb 2023).
  - The individual fee for designating Israel has been 459 CHF for one class and 345 CHF for each additional class since 18 Mar 2025. — [WIPO Information Notice 2025/2](https://wipo.int/documents/d/madrid-system/information-notices-en-2025-madrid_2025_2_e.pdf)
- Conflicting claim: one INTA-hosted source reportedly says an Israeli application cannot serve as the basis for a Madrid application. This contradicts Israel's Madrid membership (tmarkmetric). Treat it as unverified or wrong. — [INTA](https://www.inta.org/?p=17483); [tmarkmetric](https://tmarkmetric.com/guides/israel-trademark)

### Inferences
- Rough official-fee budget, before attorney fees:
  - Israel, 2 classes: about ₪2,800 to 3,150.
  - Madrid: 653 CHF basic fee plus individual fees for the US, EU and others. The US and EU individual fees are each likely several hundred CHF per class, but this is unverified; use the WIPO calculator.
  - A direct USPTO filing instead: $700 for 2 classes if ID Manual terms are used.
- Using pre-approved ID Manual descriptions (e.g., "downloadable computer operating system software"; "providing temporary use of non-downloadable AI software") avoids the $200 per class surcharge.
- Filing in Israel first and then claiming Paris priority via Madrid within 6 months fixes the priority date internationally at low cost. A centrally attacked Madrid base, however, can take down all designations for 5 years ("central attack"). This is background knowledge and should be verified.
- Practitioner fees for a full search with an opinion, and for prosecution, were not found in sources. Market rates must come from quotes.

### Gaps
- The official 2026 ILPO fee schedule (gov.il) was not retrieved, and the figures conflict.
- The current Madrid individual fees for the US and EU (2026) were not retrieved.
- EUIPO direct filing fees (EUTM) were not retrieved.
- Costs of a full commercial search (CompuMark, Corsearch) and attorney fees were not found.

## Key Question 5: Mitigations: composite marks ("LYLA OS" plus logo), coined words, alternative strategies

### Takeaway
Adding generic or descriptive words ("OS", "AI") or a logo does little to avoid confusion, because the distinctive word is what consumers remember and compare. A coined, invented word is the strongest and safest option. If LYLA is kept, the risk-reducers are: avoid AI-assistant persona and Spider-Verse associations, run a full search, and file early in Israel with a Madrid extension.

### Cited Findings
- Marks are compared as wholes, by appearance, sound, connotation and commercial impression. — [BitLaw TMEP 1207.01](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1207_01.html); [DWO triple test](https://dwo.co.il/?p=17301)
- Adding a distinguishing element did not help in PAPYROS BY MODUS v PAPYRUS. The house-mark addition "BY MODUS" did not prevent confusion for software, because PAPYROS/PAPYRUS were phonetically and conceptually close. — [ie-forum, Sept 2026](https://ie-forum.nl/artikelen/gerecht-verwarringsgevaar-tussen-papyros-by-modus-en-ouder-merk-papyrus-voor-software-en-it-diensten)
- The Firefox lesson: after two failed names, Mozilla chose a new name and carried out careful research before registering it. — [Stanford CS case study](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/cs181/projects/firefox-market-dynamics/index.html)
- A weak (crowded) name can still register, but gives narrow protection. — [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/worried-your-trademark-might-be-commercially-and-conceptually-weak-recent-opinions-by-the-trademark-trial-and-appeal-board-indicate-that-could-be-advantageous/)

### Inferences
- "LYLA OS" or "LYLA AI": "OS" and "AI" are descriptive or generic for the goods, so the dominant element stays LYLA. Composite word marks will not avoid a prior LYLA/LAYLA mark. A distinctive logo helps register a figurative mark, but it does not protect the word in use.
- Options, from lowest to highest risk:
  - (a) A fully coined word (Firefox, Kodak-style), checked across classes 9/42 and app stores.
  - (b) A distinctive compound that includes Lyla but is not merely Lyla plus a generic word (e.g., a coined prefix or suffix). This is only as safe as the search shows.
  - (c) Plain "LYLA" for an OS without an assistant persona.
  - (d) "LYLA" marketed as a personal AI assistant. This is the highest risk, because it overlaps with Marvel's character and possibly the Layla AI app.
- Practical extras:
  - Secure the domain and social handles.
  - Avoid stylization echoing Marvel (hologram, Spider-Verse fonts).
  - Document independent derivation (e.g., a family name origin), which helps rebut bad-faith arguments.
  - Budget for a possible rebrand before scaling.

### Gaps
- No case law was found specifically on whether "X OS" is distinguishable from "X" for software. The inference rests on general principles.
- No legal sources were found on naming best practice beyond the precedents above.
